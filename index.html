01 / The investigation

Two water stories. One investigative method.

We begin with monthly GRACE / GRACE-FO-derived anomalies in the project CSVs, then ask what independent evidence is needed to interpret them. The two source files do not represent identical geographic units, so this is an exploratory comparison—not a controlled like-for-like experiment.

Case A · California, USA

Central Valley

Long-term decline

The project CSV shows a persistent negative long-term signal in both total-water and groundwater anomalies. Independent research on the Central Valley documents groundwater depletion and land subsidence. These are complementary evidence layers, not proof that every local impact is visible in GRACE.

Literature benchmark: Liu et al. (2022), 15.7 ± 1.4 mm/year for groundwater depletion (2003–2021). This published estimate is not the same calculation as the live CSV trend.

Case B · Sahel/Sahara

Taoudeni–Tanezrouft Basin

A much weaker signal

The second project CSV is labelled Taoudeni–Tanezrouft Basin. Its exported series shows near-flat total-water storage and a slightly negative groundwater trend. It must not be presented as a direct measurement of the Inner Niger Delta.

The published Inner Niger Delta estimate in the research package (+7.3 to +9.5 mm/year) is a separate study result, not a result calculated from this Taoudeni–Tanezrouft CSV.

Critical distinction: the published Inner Niger Delta study and the project's Taoudeni–Tanezrouft CSV are not interchangeable. We keep their findings separate, disclose geographic uncertainty and do not infer causation, ownership or responsibility from a satellite trend alone.

03 / Our method

From detection to investigation

The same analytical sequence is applied to both regions. Every conclusion should be traceable to a source and should communicate uncertainty.

DETECT

Identify the water-storage trends in NASA satellite records.

COMPARE

Use consistent regional definitions and comparable methods.

VERIFY

Cross-check with independent satellite, scientific and public evidence.

EXPLORE

Investigate who controls resources, who benefits and who bears risk.

05 / Accessibility by design

Evidence must be safe to access

The experience is designed around keyboard access, readable structure, reduced motion, text alternatives and data tables alongside visualizations. We avoid flashing and strobing effects. A manual Safe Mode is available on this page; it does not replace testing with assistive technologies or clinical review.

Reduced motion

Respects the operating system's prefers-reduced-motion setting and removes non-essential animation.

Multiple ways to read data

Charts and maps must be paired with structured tables and concise text descriptions. Color is never the only signal.
