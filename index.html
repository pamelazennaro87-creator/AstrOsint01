Earth in Transition.
Who controls the change?

Two regions. One satellite record. Opposite water futures. AstrOsint01 turns NASA Earth-observation data into an open, accessible investigation of environmental change and its human consequences.

A trend line tells us what is changing. An investigation asks who benefits, who bears the risk, and what independent evidence supports the conclusion.

01 / The investigation

Opposite water futures

We compare terrestrial water storage (TWS) signals from NASA GRACE / GRACE-FO with independent physical, economic and human-system evidence. The figures below are reported in the project's research package; consult the source document for periods, methods and limitations.

Case A · California, USA

Central Valley

−15.7 ± 1.4 mm/year

Published groundwater-depletion estimate for 2003–2021. The investigation connects long-term storage loss with intensive pumping, land subsidence and agricultural water use.

Reported by Liu et al. (2022), as summarized in the research package. GRACE regional signals require complementary, finer-scale evidence.

Case B · Sahel, Mali

Inner Niger Delta

+7.3 to +9.5 mm/year

Positive terrestrial-water-storage trend reported for April 2002–September 2022. Increased water availability can coexist with seasonal flood exposure and unequal vulnerability.

Reported by Fatolazadeh & Goïta (2025), as summarized in the research package. Regional boundaries and uncertainty matter.

Evidence discipline: satellite observations do not, by themselves, prove ownership, causation or individual responsibility. We separate measured signals, independent corroboration, interpretation and open questions.
02 / Our method

From detection to investigation

The same analytical sequence is applied to both regions. Every conclusion should be traceable to a source and should communicate uncertainty.

DETECT

Identify the water-storage trends in NASA satellite records.

COMPARE

Use consistent regional definitions and comparable methods.

VERIFY

Cross-check with independent satellite, scientific and public evidence.

EXPLORE

Investigate who controls resources, who benefits and who bears risk.

04 / Accessibility by design

Evidence must be safe to access

The experience is designed around keyboard access, readable structure, reduced motion, text alternatives and data tables alongside visualizations. We avoid flashing and strobing effects. A manual Safe Mode is available on this page; it does not replace testing with assistive technologies or clinical review.

Reduced motion

Respects the operating system's prefers-reduced-motion setting and removes non-essential animation.

Multiple ways to read data

Charts and maps must be paired with structured tables and concise text descriptions. Color is never the only signal.
