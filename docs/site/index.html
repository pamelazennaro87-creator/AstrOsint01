The Story We Tell

Most teams show what is changing.

We show who decides what that change means.

Core Insight

In California’s Central Valley, water is extracted and converted into exportable virtual water under concentrated institutional control.

In the Inner Niger Delta, water is increasing — bringing both opportunity and elevated flood risk under local and contested control.

We do not ask users to look at Earth system trends.
We ask them to investigate who controls the change.

Two Opposite Futures

Case A

California Central Valley

Strong groundwater depletion

  • 15.7 ± 1.4 mm/year (2003–2021)
  • Permanent land subsidence confirmed by InSAR
  • High ownership concentration (top 10% hold 73–86% of land in key counties)
  • Water converted into virtual water exports

Control structure: Concentrated institutional / corporate

Case B

Inner Niger Delta (Sahel)

Significant TWS increase

  • +7.3 to +9.5 mm/year (2002–2022)
  • New water bodies and rising wells
  • Partial recovery of Lake Chad
  • Elevated flood risk and displacement

Control structure: Predominantly local / contested
